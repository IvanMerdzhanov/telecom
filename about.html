OUR STORY

From One City.
To Every City.

Orbiqa was founded in 1999 by a team of telecommunications engineers who believed that reliable connectivity was a right, not a privilege. Starting as a regional operator in the Pacific Northwest with just 12 employees and a single mobile licence, we built our first 3G network while the rest of the industry was still debating whether mobile internet would ever catch on.

Through the 2000s and 2010s, we expanded aggressively — not through acquisition of other networks, but through the painstaking construction of our own infrastructure. We laid our own fiber, built our own towers, and established our own data centres on six continents. By 2015, Orbiqa had become the first operator to offer seamless roaming across all seven continents. By 2020, we were the world's most connected network by geographic coverage. By 2024, we launched 5G Ultra — the fastest commercially available mobile network ever built.

Today, Orbiqa connects over 4.2 billion devices every single day. We carry the world's conversations, transactions, medical data, financial markets, and memories. We take that responsibility seriously. Every investment decision, every infrastructure build, every hire is guided by a single question: does this make the world more connected?

SUSTAINABILITY

Built to Last.
Built Responsibly.

Connectivity without conscience is just noise. Orbiqa is committed to being the world's most sustainable telecom operator by 2030.

100% Renewable Energy by 2030
We've already transitioned 67% of our global network to renewable energy sources. Our new data centres are powered exclusively by solar and wind. We're on track for 100% by 2028 — two years ahead of schedule.
Carbon Neutral Operations
Orbiqa has been carbon neutral since 2022, achieved through a combination of renewable energy, equipment efficiency improvements, and certified carbon offset projects in 14 countries. We're targeting carbon negative by 2027.
Digital Inclusion Programme
We've connected 12 million people in underserved communities through subsidised connectivity programmes in 45 countries. Our target: 50 million people by 2030, bridging the digital divide one connection at a time.
